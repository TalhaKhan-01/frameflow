Keep a single ending beat after the payoff (one closing question or one practical takeaway, not both stacked) and a short tail (about 10 s or less); fold any extra stat into the scene before. Never write a follow call to action: the pipeline's end card adds it.
Make the script flow for a first-time viewer: every part should lead into the next, and when the story moves on, the viewer should know what is being answered now and why. Use whatever fits the moment (a spoken question, a 'but' or 'so', a callback, a quick bridge) and vary it; never just jump to an answer the viewer wasn't set up for.
Give each scene only one new fact, and when the video runs longer, add a bridge line that links the previous fact to the next instead of adding another fact.
Before any later line corrects, answers or follows up on a claim (e.g. "And the savings?", "if it orders cheaper tests"), state that claim in plain words in an earlier scene. The viewer must have heard the claim before the rebuttal names it.
Re-hook the viewer at least every 10 seconds of narration: restate the open question, tease the next reveal, or name the stake again. No stretch of spoken narration may run longer than about 10 s without a hook line.
Keep every spoken number and every quoted phrase whole inside one caption chunk (e.g. "304 HARD CASES", "CUE EVERY 'AI'"); split captions only at phrase boundaries that never break a number or quote.
Introduce every technical term (token, weights, index, API) in a plain spoken sentence before the first line that uses it or prices it. The viewer should hear the word explained before any question about it or any number attached to it.
Give the central concept at least as much narration time as any side detour that supports the story. Before final cut, time both; if a detour runs longer, cut it to one line and fold it into the adjacent scene.
