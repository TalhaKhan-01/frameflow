Never leave a frame near-empty while the narrator is speaking: every key phrase (stat, rating, quote intro) needs its visual on screen from the first frame and held until the line ends. Check keyframes at each narration cue for blank pages.
Time each reveal visual to its narration cue and in the storyboard order. Show the big number, the object or the quote card on the cue word, hold it until the line ends, and don't show later text early.
Keep the picture in step with the voice: headings, labels and on-screen questions should match what is being said at that moment and help the viewer follow where the story is going, not introduce a question or point the narration never makes.
Before final cut, check that no two on-screen labels overlap and no stamp or sticker covers a label that names a key subject. Move or shrink the overlay until every label reads clearly on the frame where it appears.
Every object the narration names as a running thread (money, bills, score sheets, price tags) must be drawn on screen in each scene that mentions it. If a scene says a bill or cost, show the bill prop in that scene; never rely on captions alone to carry it.
Vary the diagram layout from scene to scene: never reuse the same node row or three-box template in more than two scenes. When a scene is about people (doctors, generalists, patients), draw them as people at desks with score bars that rise and sag, not as identical boxes or icons.
Give the headline stat its own dominant visual: show the big number large on screen on the cue word and keep both compared numbers visible together until the line ends, with props that make the setting readable (e.g. two labelled desks with score sheets). Never leave the key number tiny or absent.
Change the on-screen note or visual on every clause of the narration, and don't let one scene's overlay fall back to a single note. Check that no stretch over 2.5 s repeats the same frame, especially the opening seconds, and that no blank frame sits before the end card.
