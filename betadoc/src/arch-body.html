Architecture · the complete technical flow

The newsroom,opened up.

Frameflow runs on one laptop. A Telegram bot ticks every 30 seconds and moves each posting day through a small state machine. Long steps run as their own processes. Every day it makes one episode of The Daily Habit: eight stories in about 85 seconds, read by Habit. Every step is a desk with a gate, and the gates sit before the one expensive step, the render. The model calls go through Claude Code inside a subscription, so there is no API key and no API bill. This page follows the stories from the wire to the feed, and then back into the next episode through the coach, a learning loop that tests rules and keeps the ones that raise the scores.

01 · The clock

A posting day is a state machine.

One row per posting day in SQLite holds its state. The bot runs daily tick every 30 s: at 14:00 it scouts tomorrow, at 15:00 it auto-picks if nobody tapped, at 11:30 it posts what was approved, and 48 h later it collects stats. Every long step is spawned as its own process with its own log, so the bot keeps answering while a video renders, and a restart resumes from the stored state.

  1. topic-sent14:00 · 15 candidates on the topic card
  2. pickedowner ticks 5–8, or the top 8 at 15:00
  3. makingdaily make: research → rundown → hook card → visuals → voice → one render
  4. video-sentvideo card: Approve · Revise · Reject
  5. queuedapproved; waits for the 11:30 slot
  6. postingpack → YouTube, Instagram, Facebook
  7. postedlinks back to Telegram, Studio refreshed
  8. +48 h statsstats → coach → library → cleanup

side exits: revising → making (at most 2) · skipped · rejected (the reason goes to the coach) · waiting-limit (a usage limit: re-run after the reset) · failed (/retry)

02 · Intake

Hundreds of headlines, one ranked card.

ideas collect pulls the wire (research/sources.json) into data/frameflow.db, deduplicated by URL: tech feeds, Google News searches and topic pages, and Google Trends for India, the US, the UK, Singapore, the UAE and Australia. There are no lane limits: anything trending anywhere can make the show if it has a tech, money or science angle Habit can explain. ideas rank scores every fresh idea in one batched model call. Topic memory then drops any story aired in the last 14 days (by meaning, not by link), and the topic desk ranks the rest by virality and sends 15 to Telegram as toggles.

03 · Research and fact-check

Nothing unsourced reaches the script.

One researcher per story (four at a time) works with web search and fetch and returns facts that each carry the URL that supports them. Then code takes over, because a model can be confidently wrong:

  1. Re-fetch. Every source is loaded again. A fact whose page doesn't load is dropped.
  2. Fresh pages. A page whose link is dated more than 21 days before the episode is never shown on screen.
  3. Independent check. The shorts-factcheck skill re-reads each page as text and rules on each fact from that text alone: supported, partly (with corrected wording), unsupported (dropped) or unreachable.
  4. Numbers in code. Later, every number the script says, in digits or in words, must be in the verified facts.

04 · The desks of a daily make

Eighteen desks, one render.

A make goes desk by desk, and each desk's gate must pass before the next one starts. Fixes are allowed only while a fix is still a text edit. After the render nothing loops: the episode goes to the owner with its honest numbers, and the coach takes its lessons.

05 · The Daily Habit

Eight stories, one episode.

An episode is an open, up to eight segments of 7.5–10.5 seconds and an outro, 75–100 seconds in all. Each segment starts on its real visuals (the subject's photo, then the source's headline zooming in under a highlighter) and moves to its graphic, with Habit in a split shot or in the corner, a countdown into the next story, and the topic strip and source chip on top. The look is one asset swap: the paper desk set and the drawn Habit by default, the pixel Habit on the end card.

What changed in November

  • The coach. The old lessons ledger is gone. The coach treats every episode as an experiment: the proven rules plus two rules under test go into the writer's and the visual desk's prompts, and the review decides which survive (chapter 09).
  • World trends, no lanes. Google Trends from six countries joins the wire; any trending story with a tech, money or science angle can make the eight.
  • A fairer bar. The screening judge varies by a point or two between runs, so it runs twice and the bar uses the mean.
  • Honest pictures. The BREAKING banner always names the story the open is about; a page dated weeks before the episode is never shown; poster text is hidden when a close-up would cut it.
  • New graphics and depth. Maps from Natural Earth (public domain) with a route and a vehicle riding it; the back set moves slower than Habit when the camera pushes in.
  • The voice. A pace target per line, names respelled for the voice only, and at most one real laugh an episode.
  • Still from October: topic memory, the hook card, real visuals with licences, and analogy gags from a fixed catalogue (a chip the size of a rice grain, one picture twelve times, a photo punchline, a stamp).

06 · The render engine

Six layers become one frame.

An episode is a show plan: JSON with every segment's start, shots, graphic, media, gag and Habit's face, plus the word timings. show.py puts it into one HyperFrames composition (scenes/_shared/show/, 1080×1920, GSAP) and the renderer steps it frame by frame. Nothing depends on the wall clock, and everything that moves is placed by position or an SVG transform, so two renders of the same plan give identical frames.

  1. Show planThe director's JSON; hyperframes check must report 0 layout errors.
  2. CompositionThe paper desk set, the drawn Habit (8 faces, mouth from the voice), graphics, the parallax back set, bundled OFL fonts.
  3. VoiceChatterbox on the GPU, seeded; the show never swaps voices. Each line's emotion sets its expression and its pace.
  4. CaptionsWhisper word timings → phrase chunks; the spoken word in red, the stressed words in blue.
  5. SoundStamp, theme, a bed that rises and ducks under the voice, a dip and a hit before the outro, the SFX cue sheet.
  6. MasterLoudness to −14 LUFS, true peak under −1 dBTP; an asset ledger row per file.

07 · Quality gates

The file is measured, not admired.

One file, research/quality-bar.json, holds every number, and every desk reads it, so no two desks aim at different targets. bar calibrate re-measures the voice's real speed (2.89 words a second today) after each video and copies it to the writer and director. Below, a scanner passes over 12 real frames of the latest episode while the gates resolve.

  • Blankno near-empty frame ≥ 1 s

  • Captionsword error < 10%, numbers read as said
  • Layout0 check errors, nothing over text
  • Licencesa ledger row for every asset
  • Freshno page dated > 21 days
  • Bannernames the open's story

Then the judgement rows, 0–2 each, by models that never wrote the episode: the table read (hook, clarity, wit, flow, accuracy, variety, ending) and the screening of the stills (look, readability, framing, captions, package, story, Habit), run twice and averaged. The bar is 80% of 28 with no row under 1. A miss is not hidden: the card says what failed, "Approve anyway" is the owner's call, and the coach aims its next rules at the weakest rows.

09 · The learning loop: the coach

Every episode is an experiment.

  • Rules, not lessons. The playbook (research/coach.json) holds short, general rules for the writer and the visual desk. Each one aims at one review row (accuracy, readability, story…) and carries a belief that it helps, a Beta distribution that starts at 50/50.
  • Thompson sampling. Before a make, the proven rules and two rules under test go into the prompts. The two are drawn from their beliefs, so promising rules get tried more and weak ones less.
  • Credit against a baseline. After the review, each rule's row is compared with that row's running average over earlier episodes. Above it: credit. Below: blame. Within a quarter point: half of each.
  • Promote and retire. After three or more trials, a rule that helps with 85% confidence becomes proven; one at 25% or less, or still unclear after six trials, is retired. A proven rule that stops working goes back under test.
  • Two speeds. The review is the fast signal; 48 h later, the episode's % watched against the channel's median gives its rules half a point more. The owner's words (Revise, Reject, /feedback) become new rules with a head start.
  • Practice between makes. coach practice re-writes a past episode with and without a rule under test and table-reads both: a cheap extra trial with no render.
  • Tickets. Rows the words can't move (look, framing, captions, Habit) become engineering tickets for the template, never prompt rules.

10 · Model routing

The big model only where quality is decided.

Every call goes through llm.py: claude -p with no tools unless asked, no project settings, a short system prompt and structured JSON out. Token usage is appended to data/llm-usage.jsonl, so the cost of every video can be measured. The show's routing is in research/show.json:

Opus where quality is decided

  • the rundown writer + doctor
  • the table read
  • the screening (× 2)

Sonnet medium effort

  • research per story
  • virality ranking
  • the hook room
  • the coach's new rules
  • post captions

Haiku small jobs

  • idea ranking
  • visual cues
  • pronunciation list
  • card post text

Measured, not guessed: most of a fix round's output was the model thinking (60k tokens to change a few scenes), so fixes moved to Sonnet at medium effort. A usage limit pauses the step and re-runs it after the reset.

11 · Infrastructure

Three processes and one on call, two static sites.

frameflow-botTelegram long polling (no webhook, so no public URL) and the 30 s daily tick. Spawns every long step as its own process.
frameflow-chromeThe Pilot's dedicated Chrome, signed in to the platforms, DevTools on localhost only. Started for an upload and stopped after it (a lock keeps two posts from closing it under each other), so its 1.4 GB stay free the rest of the day.
frameflow-controlA local control panel with the same functions as the bot, plus a live Making panel fed by progress.py.
phasedoc-frameflowThe build cockpit: every task in the plan with an acceptance test that has actually run.

All four are registered with a process manager that restores them at boot and restarts them after a crash; Chrome is kept stopped between posts. Python runs in one uv environment; Chatterbox runs in a throwaway environment of its own because PyTorch is too heavy for the project. Secrets live in one mode-600 file outside the repository.

From the laptop to the web

  1. web exportsnapshot of runs, episodes, the coach, library, build
  2. open | lockplain studio.json, or AES-GCM studio.enc (PBKDF2-SHA256, 600,000 rounds)
  3. git pushonly web/public and betadoc/public
  4. Vercel × 2Studio + this betadoc, built on every push
  5. browserlocked mode: the PIN derives the key and decrypts in the page

Only media that is already public is copied. Paths, tokens and chat ids never leave the machine. scripts/deploy-check.sh waits for the Vercel build of HEAD and probes the live site.
