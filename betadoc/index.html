Night edition · an automated newsroom

One story a day.Written, drawn, voiced,checked and postedon its own.

Frameflow is the engine behind The Daily Habit Studio, a Thirdspace Production. Every day it makes The Daily Habit, a 75–100 second news show where Habit covers 5–8 stories about tech, AI and money, with the real logos, photos and headlines on screen. It posts to YouTube, Instagram and Facebook at 11:30. The owner ticks the stories, picks the hook and taps Approve on Telegram.

Thirdspace House · home of The Daily Habit Studio

Every desk is a person. Every floor is at work.

Frameflow runs like a small production house. Each role on its board is a desk with its own check, and no desk grades its own work. Here is the house replaying its latest make: the lift carries the script folder up as the work climbs. Scroll to go floor by floor, or tap any floor to go inside.

  1. Ground · The NewsroomThe researcher pulls the story off the wire and writes a sourced brief. The fact-checker re-reads every source and stamps what holds up.
  2. 1st · The Writers' RoomPackaging sets the title, cover line and hook promise first. The writer drafts in Habit's voice, the director storyboards beats on words, and a script editor holds an independent table read.
  3. 2nd · The BoothHabit records. Timing is checked on the real voice: a re-hook every 10 s, nothing too fast. Standards checks labels, licences and every number on screen.
  4. 3rd · The Screening RoomA 12 fps animatic is drawn and screened in full before anything expensive happens. Fix rounds happen here, on the cheap draft.
  5. 4th · PostOne delivery render, then QC: loudness, black frames, motion, captions.
  6. 5th · The CabinTalha gets the video on Telegram: approve, revise in his own words, or reject. The showrunner turns a revise into a change order.
  7. The Roof · DispatchAt 11:30 the publisher posts to YouTube, Instagram and Facebook and checks each post. The analyst reads the 48 h numbers back into the next make.

The problem

A daily Short is a daily job.

Find a story worth telling today. Check the facts. Write a hook that holds people past the first two seconds. Draw it, voice it, cut it, mix it. Then post it three times, with three sets of captions.

Do that by hand and the habit breaks in a week. Skip the checks and you post something wrong. Hand it to a generic AI tool and every video looks the same.

    Architecture

    One laptop, a few model calls, a lot of craft.

    Models write, direct, check and screen. Everything else (voice, music, sound effects, rendering, timing checks) runs locally and deterministically, so a scene renders the same way every time.

    Deployment

    Where it runs.

    The newsroom: one laptop

    The bot, the daily scheduler and a dedicated browser for posting run under a process manager and come back after reboots. The laptop is on 10:00–17:00; a step stopped by a shutdown restarts itself, and a usage limit just waits for its reset.

    The Studio: static, open for now

    After each post the pipeline exports a snapshot and pushes it. Paths, tokens and chat ids never leave the machine, and media appears only once it is public. A PIN lock (AES-GCM, key from PBKDF2) can be switched back on with one command.

    No cloud twin, on purpose

    Rendering and posting need the owner's signed-in accounts and a few hours of compute a day, so Frameflow runs where those live. The Studio is the safe window onto it.
